What is a program? 
- Consists of modules
- that are made up of functions
- that contain blocks
- of instructions
- that operate on variables and data structures

Modules are collections of functions. Manuals of libraries will allow us to focus our reversing efforts on the individual programs.

Functions represent well-encapsulated functionality.
Most functions will have a defined goal
- Like set some data
- calculate/retrieve/validate data
- dispatch other functions
- perform some action on the outside world via syscalls. 
They can be reverse engineered individually then pieced together later.

Functions can be represented as a graph. Each block of instructions will execute one after the other. Blocks are joined by edges, representing conditional and unconditional jumps. By understanding what the blocks do and what conditions trigger what edges you can understand the functions logic.
